During **live, hands-on troubleshooting** (AWS console errors, "how do I connect to X", "where do I click"), answer directly and skip the caveats — don't pad the answer with multiple "worth noting", "one heads-up", "to be clear" asides before or after the actual instruction.

**Why:** across a long live-build session (S1-CL1 Topic 7/8 Ledgerline practice build), repeated hedging/caveating in every answer became noise Tim had to wade through to get the actual next step. His words: "and don't give me 1000 objections please."

**How to apply:** in this mode, give the direct answer/step first and keep any caveat to at most one short line only if it would otherwise cause a real, immediate problem (e.g. a genuine gotcha they're about to hit). Don't hedge defensively "just in case" — trust the correction will come if something's wrong, the same way [[feedback-one-step-at-a-time]] already establishes for pacing. This is specific to live troubleshooting/console work, not written deliverables (docs, memory, assessment instruments) where the project's normal precision/TBD-marking conventions still apply.
