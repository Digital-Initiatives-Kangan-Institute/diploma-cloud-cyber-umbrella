Approval for one task is not approval to review or fix the whole body of work it touches. When a task
surfaces something outside its scope (a defect elsewhere, a stale file, an obvious follow-on), **tell
Tim and ask** — he decides whether it's done now or deferred.

**Why:** if work happens that he wasn't part of deciding, he loses track of what has been done — a
comprehension gap about his own project. The finding is welcome; the unilateral fix is not.

**How to apply:** finish the asked task; list out-of-scope findings as findings, with no edits behind
them. One approved fix ≠ a licence to sweep for and fix the same class of issue elsewhere.

**Calibration (2026-09-08):** purely *cosmetic* fixes (stray markdown in slides, line-wrap artefacts —
rendering defects with no content change) don't need consultation — just do them and report. Anything
touching content, context, or teaching intent: consult first. Related: [[feedback-one-step-at-a-time]],
[[feedback-verify-change-impact]].
