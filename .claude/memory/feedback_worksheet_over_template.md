The preferred position: **everything the student produces is captured in the assessment worksheet
itself** (the run-sheet instrument). A separate fillable template is used **only** where a unit-of-
competency requirement explicitly and definitely calls for one — not because a deliverable of that
kind has historically been a document.

**Why:** a large blank template asks the student to hold the whole shape of an unfamiliar document in
their head before they can start. The worksheet walks them through it one framed item at a time, so
the thinking is scaffolded and the evidence lands where the task that produced it sits. A template
that no UoC item requires is work that evidences nothing.

**How to apply:**
- Author the worksheet first and assume it is the whole deliverable.
- Before keeping (or building) a template, find the UoC item that requires a separate document and
  quote it. Worked contrast in S1-CL2 AT1: `[ICTCLD501 AC 3]` *"reporting standards for documenting
  and communicating disaster recovery plan"* → the DR plan **is** a separate document; `[ICTCLD503]`
  ACs 1–9 are all environment/input conditions and name no document format, so
  `[ICTCLD503 PC 1.7]` / `[PC 2.4]` *"document and justify architecture design"* are satisfied by the
  worksheet itself.
- Where a template survives, the worksheet still does the generative work and its closing task copies
  the answers across into the template; both are submitted.
- **Exemplars follow templates.** The assessor worksheet carries the model answers, so an exemplar
  exists only where a student-fillable template survives. Exemplars with no student submittable under
  the revised design are removed.
- Legacy content is not authoritative — trace it to a UoC item or drop it. Watch for the two failure
  modes found in S1-CL1: assessing what no item requires, and setting a bar well above what a vague
  item actually asks for. See [[feedback-mark-uoc-intent-not-invented-settings]].
