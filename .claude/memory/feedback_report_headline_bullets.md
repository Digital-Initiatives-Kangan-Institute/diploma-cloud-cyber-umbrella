When reporting what has been done, use **headline, then summary heading, then two or three bullets**;
another summary heading, another two or three bullets. Bullets are only added where they earn it — a
heading often stands alone.

**Never a wall of text.** A big block of prose forces Tim to hunt for what matters, and in practice he
reads the first couple of sentences and stops — so anything important below that is lost. Length is not
the problem; undifferentiated shape is. The headings are what let him skim to the part he needs.

**How to apply:**
- Open with the outcome in one line.
- Then `**A short heading**` per distinct thing, with 2–3 bullets under it at most.
- One idea per bullet. If a heading needs more than three bullets, it is really two headings.
- Drop anything that does not change what he does next — see [[feedback-checks-inform-not-decide]] for
  the related habit of not padding with narration.
- Applies to *reports on work done*. Answering a question is still prose — see the output style.
- **When unsure how much detail is wanted, give the summary and ask "want more detail?"** — never
  default to the full explanation. The short version plus an offer costs him nothing; the long version
  costs him the hunt for what mattered.
